\section*{Study of the convergence parameters}


In order to plot the HHG-C spectra, 
we currently rely on different formula. One of them reads 
\begin{equation}
Q(\omega) = \left|-\dot{d}(\omega) \right|^2, \;\;\;{\rm Eq. (22)}\label{eqn:diff_dipole}
\end{equation}

\noindent where the dipole moment $d(t)$ is given by

\begin{equation}
d(t)= - \int \psi^*(x,t)\,\hat{x}\,\psi(x,t)dx, \label{eqn:dipole_moment}
\end{equation}

\noindent and its Fourier transform, by the relation

\begin{equation}
d(\omega) = \int e^{i\omega t} d(t)dt. \label{eqn:fourier}
\end{equation}

In order to deal with the derivative in Eq. (\ref{eqn:dipole_moment}),
we currently use the following formula


\begin{align}
f^{\prime} (x_i) = & \frac{f^{\prime}_l (x_i) + f^{\prime}_r (x_i)}{2}
\label{eqn:diff} 
\end{align}

\noindent with

\begin{align}
f^{\prime}_l (x_i) = \frac{f(x_{i+1}) -  f(x_i)}{x_{i+1} -x_i}, & \hspace{20pt}
f^{\prime}_r (x_i) = \frac{f(x_{i}) -  f(x_{i-1})}{x_{i} - x_{i-1}}. \nonumber
\end{align}

Let us note that a dot express differentiation in time.
In this document we want to evaluate how accurate is the approximation 
expressed by Eq. (\ref{eqn:diff}).

\section{Test on usual, continuous and differentiable function}

In this section, we want to see how accurately 
Eq. (\ref{eqn:diff}) approximates the derivqative of a smooth an differentiable 
function. The function we are using for this test is  $f(x) = \sin (x)$.
So $f^\prime(x) = \cos (x)$. Fig. (\ref{fig:err}) shows the relative error 
between the analytical formula and the numerical estimation,
according to the formula

\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{\rm rel} = \left| \frac{f^\prime (x) - \cos (x)}{\cos(x)} \right|.
\end{equation}

Let us recall that $f(x) = \sin (x)$ here.
The precision depends on the number of steps 
\begin{equation}
  \Delta x = (b - a)/ N, \hspace{40pt} b = -a = \pi.
\end{equation}

As shown in the figure, the approximation expressed in Eq. (\ref{eqn:diff})
is of order 2, except on the boundaries.

\section{Test on the fundamental of the zero range potential}

We now want to test the accuracy on a more difficult function, that is
\begin{equation}
\psi_0(x) = \varkappa^{1/2} e^{-\varkappa \left|x\right|}.
\end{equation}

The derivative of this function will then be 
\begin{equation}
\psi_0^\prime (x) = \begin{cases}
                    \ \ \varkappa^{3/2} e^{\varkappa x} 
                & \text{if $x \geqslant 0$,} \\
                    -\varkappa^{3/2} e^{-\varkappa x} 
                & \text{if $x \leqslant 0$. } 
\end{cases}
\label{eqn:deriv}
\end{equation}



We want to know how well Eq. (\ref{eqn:diff}) approximates
Eq. (\ref{eqn:deriv}).
As can be seen in Fig. (\ref{fig:err2}), the approximation is not as good 
as in the case of differentiable functions.
Tab (\ref{tab:err2}) and Tab (\ref{tab:err3}) show the 
evolution of the relative error around $x=0$ for space grids.

\section{Accuracy of Eq. (\ref{eqn:diff}) for evaluating the
derivative of $\psi(x,t)$}

The final test we want to perform is the comparison of the formula
\begin{align}
    Q^{(c)}(\omega) = \left| p(\omega)  \right|^2;\;\; {\rm Eq. (21)} \label{eqn:eq104}
\end{align}

\noindent with $\hat{p} = -id/dx$, and $p(t)$ and $p(\omega)$ evaluated 
acccording to Eq. (\ref{eqn:dipole_moment}) and (\ref{eqn:fourier}),
to Eq. (\ref{eqn:diff_dipole}).
Moreover, we also want to compare it to teh following formula

\begin{align}
    Q^{(c)}(\omega) = 
    = \left|\Bigg[ e^{i\omega t} x(t)\Bigg]_{-T/2}^{T/2}
               -i\omega \int_{-T/2}^{T/2} e^{i\omega t}x(t)dt\right|^2, \;\;\rm Eq.(31)  \label{eqn:eq31}
\end{align}

\noindent refered to as Eq. (31).

Eq. (\ref{eqn:eq104}) is designated by Eq. (21) in a previous document 
we submitted. The results are compiled in Fig. (\ref{fig:eq104_test}).
The two last panels with intensities $F_0 = 0.3\,\rm a.u.$ show how 
Eq. (\ref{eqn:eq104}) and (\ref{eqn:eq31}) compare 
to Eq. (\ref{eqn:diff_dipole}).
