dynamics changes

\small
\begin{textblock*}{250pt}(30pt,60pt)
\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item In the MP picture, excitation follows the 
$\rm Ar$ dipole selection rule
\item Authors showed elsewhere that parity effect is observed for CEP-averaged
pulses
\item It is shown here that this is true for sine and cosine pulses
\item Fig. 3(a) and (b) Distribution of $l$ states due to a cosine- 
and sine- pulses
\item Fig. 3(c) Normalized probability for same parity excitated states 
\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item[$\bullet$] At low intensity, parity effect is observed for both CEPs, 
consistent with expectation for MP model
\item[$\bullet$] For cosine pulses, parity is observed at all intensities
\item[$\bullet$] For sine pulses, parity conservation is lost for $\gamma > 1$
\end{itemize}
\item Venzke \textit{et al.} showed that parity 
can break down with few cycles pulses
\item \textbf{Parity depends on CEP for higher intensities}
\item Since cosine pulse doesn't break parity, 
spatial symmetry of the field matters in the tunneling-regime 
\end{itemize}
\end{textblock*}
