\framesubtitle{The state of the problem}

\small
\begin{textblock*}{400pt}(30pt,60pt)
\begin{itemize}
\item Conservation of momentum
\begin{align}
\footnotesize
\left\langle p_{x,e} \right\rangle + \left\langle p_{x,\rm ion} \right\rangle
 = \frac{E_\gamma}{c} 
 = \frac{E_e}{c} + \frac{I_p}{c}. \nonumber
\end{align}
\item How is the photon energy shared between electrons and ions?
\begin{itemize}
 \footnotesize
 \item[$\bullet$] For high photon energies, \hspace{10pt}
   $\left\langle p_{x,e}\right\rangle = 8/5\times E_e/c.$
 \item[$\bullet$] For recollision free strong field ionization, %\footnote{\Tiny Chelkowski \textit{et al.}, Phys. Rev. Lett. 113, 263005, (2014)}
 \hspace{10pt}
   $\left\langle p_{x,e} \right\rangle \sim E_e/c + 1/3\times I_p.$
 \item[$\bullet$] For linearly polarized fields, around $2U_p$ region, 
   \hspace{10pt}
   $\left\langle p_{x,e}\right\rangle \sim E_e/c.$
\end{itemize}
\item For rescattered electrons whose energy can reach up to $10U_p$, 
the question is open. \\
Still, Brennecke \textit{et al.} predicted that for $\rm He$,
$\langle p_{x,e}\rangle$ levels off and remains constant.
\item Some interesting comments
\begin{itemize}
 \footnotesize
% \item[$\bullet$] Recollision processes are at the heart of phenomema such as 
%HHG and laser-induced electron diffraction
 \item[$\bullet$] Requirements of high intensities and \underline{long} 
wavelengths place HHG and LIED outside of the dipole oasis.
 \item[$\bullet$] Nondipole effects have been deepened by the analysis 
of NSI, field inhomogeneity, nonadiabaticity and weak coulomb effects...
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}
\end{textblock*}

\framesubtitle{What to expect from this paper}

\small
\begin{textblock*}{400pt}(30pt,60pt)
\begin{itemize}
\item First experimental results on the photon-momentum transfer
for the ionization of $\rm Xe$ atoms by linearly polarized light 
beyond the classical cutoff.
\item First experimental test of the Brennecke proposition, 
(which was about $\rm He$).
\vspace{10pt}
\item Quick experimental observations
\begin{itemize}
\item[$\bullet$] A breakdown of $\left\langle p_{x,e} \right\rangle$
is found as soon as energy gets higher than $2U_p$.
\item[$\bullet$] Still, the pleateau-like behaviour predicted by Brennecke 
for $\rm He$ is not seen.
\item[$\bullet$] Chances are that the momentum transfer is target sensitive.
\item[$\bullet$] Present paper results are for $\rm Xe$. 
\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\end{textblock*}

\end{frame}

\framesubtitle{Modelling and theoretical calculations}

%\begin{textblock*}{100pt}(280pt,70pt)
%\includegraphics[scale=.30]{images/medium3.png}
%\end{textblock*}

\small
\begin{textblock*}{400pt}(30pt,60pt)
\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item Explanation of the local minimum structure.
\item Simple backreflection model (BRM) based on some 
nondipole generalization of the 3 steps model.
\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item[$\bullet$] First steps are the exact same.
\item[$\bullet$] Incident velocity is flipped around by the magnetic field 
and augmented by $v_{{\rm in},x} = v_{{\rm in},z}^2/(2c)$ 
along the light propagation direction.
\item[$\bullet$] For $\rm Xe^+$, the differential electron scattering 
cross-section has a local maximum at $180^{\degree}$ (backscattering) 
which induces the maximum in the $p_x$-direction near the $p_z$-axis.
\item[$\bullet$] They therefore \underline{estimate} the nondipole shift
by "assuming" that all electrons are backscattered.
\item[$\bullet$] Recollision slows down the electron momentum along $p_x$-, 
while it is boosted along $p_z$-direction.
\item[$\bullet$] Two trajectories born per optical cycle 
contribute for the same $p_z$, termed "long-" and "short-".
\end{itemize}
\item Low Frequency Approximation (LFA) is implemented in order to complete 
the above model.
\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item[$\bullet$] Based on complex-valued electron trajectories.
\item[$\bullet$] Accurately incorporate the field free differential. 
\item[$\bullet$] 
\begin{align}
M(\boldsymbol{p}) = P(\boldsymbol{p}) 
T\left[ \boldsymbol{v}_{\rm in}(\boldsymbol{p}), 
\boldsymbol{v}_{\rm out}(\boldsymbol{p})\right], 
\hspace{20pt}\sigma \sim \left|  T\right|^2. \nonumber
\end{align}
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}
\end{textblock*}

\end{frame}

\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item[$\bullet$] Nondipole shit in LFA long- and short- trajectories

\item (b) Probability of quantum orbits with final momentum $p_x = 0$ in LFA.
\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item[$\bullet$] Though both orbits contribute, the shift is strongly

\item[$\bullet$] Relative weight mainly determined by the energy-dependent

\item[$\bullet$] Long- dominates for $p_z \leqslant 1.3\,\rm a.u$, as 
short- becomes comparable for $1.3\,{\rm a.u.} 
\leqslant p_z \leqslant 1.7\,{\rm a.u.}$
\item[$\bullet$] Transition from long- to short-, then back to long trajectory 
causing the minimum structure in the shift $p_x$.
\end{itemize}
\item (c) Focal volume-averaged LFA result calculated for 
the Tong-Lin potential.
\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item[$\bullet$] Reproduces the experimentally observed minimum structure.
\item[$\bullet$] Confirms the prediction for $p_z \geqslant 1.6\,\rm a.u$.
\end{itemize}

\small
\begin{textblock*}{250pt}(30pt,60pt)
\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item Explanation of the difference between $\rm He$ and $\rm Xe$.
\vspace{10pt}
\item Model potential $V(r)$ expression
\begin{enumerate}
\footnotesize
\item
$-\left( Z + a_1 e^{-a_2 r} + a_3 e^{-a_4 r} + a_5 e^{-a_6 r} \right)/r$
(Tong-Lin~),
\item
$-2\left( N\Gamma - Z \right)/r, \;\; \mbox{with}\;\;
\Gamma = 1 - \left[ \left( e^{r/d}  \right)B + 1 \right]$ (GSZ),
\item
$-\left( Z + a_1 e^{-a_2 r} \right)/r$ (effective).
\end{enumerate}
\item Predicted shifts different in the  HATI region, which implies 
that the electron-ion interaction plays an important role.
\item Tong-Lin potential (1) gives the most accurate results.
\item Comparison shows that shifts are very sensitive to the details 
of the scattering process.
\item This also explains why results are different from $\rm He$, since 
the specificities of the target are very important for the dynamics.
\item Good agreement between theory and prediction 
for $p_z \leqslant 1.0\,\rm a.u.$
\end{itemize}
\end{textblock*}

\begin{frame}

\framesubtitle{Closure}

\small
\begin{textblock*}{400pt}(30pt,60pt)
\begin{itemize}
\footnotesize
\item Partitioning of the linear photon momentum between 
electron and nucleus altered by the rescattering process. 
\item Depending on the electron energy compared to $2U_p$, the share 
taken by the electron drastically different.
\item In the case of $\rm Xe$, domination of the distribution by 
backscattered electrons in the  $2\sqrt{U_p} \leqslant p_z$ region.
\item Recollision with the parent ion transfers 
most of the momentum to the core, which halves the momentum altogether,
especially in the $p_x$ direction. 

\vspace{20pt}

\item Sensitivity of the nondipole shift to the inner structure 
of the parent ion turns it into a promising tool 
for atomic and molecular imaging.
\end{itemize}
\end{textblock*}
